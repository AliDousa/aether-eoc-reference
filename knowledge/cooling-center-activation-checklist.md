# Cooling Centre Activation Checklist

Applies to: extreme-heat activations under a declared public-health surge.
Owning section: Operations, in coordination with Logistics.

## 1. Activation triggers

Open the **first** cooling centre in a sector when any one of the following is true:

- Forecast heat index is at or above 41 °C for six consecutive hours.
- A mass-gathering event in the sector is expected to exceed 5,000 attendees
  while the heat index is at or above 38 °C.
- Two or more heat-related presentations are reported from the same sector
  within a single operational period.

## 2. When to open a SECOND cooling centre in a sector

Open an additional cooling centre in the same sector when **any** of the
following thresholds is crossed. This is the decision point most often raised
to the Incident Commander, so the thresholds are deliberately explicit.

| # | Threshold | Measure |
|---|-----------|---------|
| C1 | **Occupancy** | The existing centre exceeds **80 % of rated capacity** for 60 continuous minutes. |
| C2 | **Turn-aways** | Any person is turned away for lack of space. This is a hard trigger — one turn-away is enough. |
| C3 | **Travel time** | Median travel time from the sector's population centre to the nearest open centre exceeds **20 minutes** on foot or by shuttle. |
| C4 | **Clinical load** | Heat-related presentations at the co-located clinic exceed **10 per hour** for two consecutive hours. |
| C5 | **Forecast** | Heat index is forecast to remain at or above 43 °C into the next operational period with occupancy already above 60 %. |

Escalation: crossing **C1 or C3 alone** is a Logistics Section decision.
Crossing **C2, C4, or two or more thresholds together** is raised to the
Incident Commander before activation, because it commits staffing that is
usually held in reserve.

## 3. Minimum opening set

A cooling centre may not be declared open until all of the following are on
site and confirmed by the site lead:

- Potable water for 4 litres per expected occupant per operational period.
- Oral rehydration salts (ORS) sufficient for 10 % of expected occupancy.
- Shade-shelter kits covering any queueing area outside the structure.
- Cots at a ratio of one per eight expected occupants.
- At least one staff member trained in heat-illness recognition on shift.
- A working means of reporting occupancy to the EOC every 30 minutes.

## 4. Occupancy reporting

Site leads report occupancy, turn-aways, and clinical referrals to the EOC on a
30-minute cadence. A missed report is treated as an amber status until the next
successful check-in; two consecutive missed reports escalate to Operations.

## 5. Demobilisation

Do not close a centre while the heat index remains at or above 38 °C. Close
only after two consecutive operational periods below that threshold with
occupancy under 25 %, and only after confirming that remaining occupants have
onward transport or shelter.
