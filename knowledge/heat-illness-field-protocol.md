# Heat Illness Field Protocol

Owning section: Operations (Medical Unit).
Applies to: field teams and cooling-centre staff during an extreme-heat activation.

## 1. Recognition

Three presentations, in increasing severity:

| Presentation | Typical signs | Core temperature |
|--------------|---------------|------------------|
| **Heat cramps** | Painful muscle spasms, heavy sweating, normal mentation | Normal |
| **Heat exhaustion** | Weakness, headache, nausea, dizziness, heavy sweating, tachycardia | Elevated, below 40 °C |
| **Heat stroke** | Altered mental status, confusion, collapse, seizure; sweating may be present or absent | At or above 40 °C |

**Altered mental status in a hot environment is heat stroke until proven
otherwise.** It is a time-critical emergency.

## 2. Immediate field actions

For all presentations:

1. Move the person out of direct sun into shade or a cooled space.
2. Remove excess clothing and equipment.
3. Begin active cooling.
4. Record time of onset and time cooling began.

For suspected **heat stroke**, additionally:

- Begin whole-body cooling immediately. Do not delay cooling for transport.
- Escalate for medical transport at once.
- Do not give oral fluids to anyone with altered mental status.

## 3. Rehydration

- Conscious, oriented, able to swallow: oral rehydration salts (ORS) in water,
  sipped steadily. One ORS sachet per 200 ml unless the packaging states
  otherwise.
- Plain water alone is inadequate for someone who has been sweating heavily
  for hours; salt replacement matters.
- Do not use caffeinated or high-sugar drinks for rehydration.
- Anyone unable to protect their airway receives nothing by mouth.

## 4. Consumption planning

For resource forecasting, plan on:

- **4 litres** of drinking water per person per operational period in shaded rest.
- **1 ORS sachet** per 10 occupants per hour during an active heat warning.
- Consumption roughly **doubles** for outdoor working teams versus seated
  occupants at a cooling centre.

These figures drive the ORS and water burn-rate projections shown on the board.
They are planning estimates, not clinical dosing.

## 5. Field team rotation

Outdoor teams rotate on a **20 minutes on / 40 minutes off** cycle when the
heat index is at or above 41 °C. Rotation is mandatory and is tracked by the
team lead. A team that has missed two consecutive rotations is stood down and
replaced.

## 6. Reporting

Every heat-illness presentation is reported to the EOC with: sector, time of
onset, presentation category, cooling start time, and disposition. Aggregate
counts by sector drive the surveillance picture and the cooling-centre
activation thresholds in `cooling-center-activation-checklist.md`.
